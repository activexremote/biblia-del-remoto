# The remote lexicon

The terms used throughout the bible, on one page, for quick reference. Each is fully defined the first time it appears in its section; this is the summary.

| **Term**                            | **In one line**                                                                                                               |
|-------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Async-first**                     | Your default mode doesn’t wait for an instant reply: you write it complete, they answer when they can. Sync is the exception. |
| **Documentation-first**             | If it isn’t written, it didn’t happen. Decisions and processes live in a document, not a head.                                |
| **Single source of truth**          | Everything that matters has one canonical home. Two versions of the truth is zero.                                            |
| **Five levels of remote**           | Mullenweg’s framework: from level 1 (nothing remote) to 5 (distributed beats in-person). The goal is 4: async by default.     |
| **ROWE (outputs, not presence)**    | You’re judged on what you produce, not hours online. The only management that survives time zones.                            |
| **Independent contractor**          | You provide a service under a commercial, not employment, contract. You invoice; you’re not an employee.                      |
| **EOR (Employer of Record)**        | A third party is your legal employer in your country and “leases” you to the client, who directs your work.                   |
| **Misclassification**               | Treating as a contractor someone the law considers an employee. The number-one legal risk of hiring abroad.                   |
| **ABC test**                        | Three conditions that decide if someone is genuinely a contractor. Fail one, and they’re an employee.                         |
| **AB5 · IR35**                      | Laws (California · UK) that chase the “disguised employee” posing as a contractor.                                            |
| **Permanent establishment (PE)**    | When your people’s activity in a country makes your company owe corporate tax there.                                          |
| **Tax residency**                   | The country with the right to tax your worldwide income. Depends on where you live, not your passport.                        |
| **183-day rule**                    | Spending 183+ days in a country usually makes you a tax resident there. A guide, not a universal law.                         |
| **Geo-tiering**                     | Salary bands by role level and geography: same role, different pay by country.                                                |
| **Proximity bias**                  | Valuing and promoting whoever is more visible, even if they don’t produce more. Punishes time zone, not work.                 |
| **Deep work**                       | Long, uninterrupted blocks where you produce what demands concentration.                                                      |
| **Overlap**                         | The window when you and the team are awake at once. Agreed; it needn’t be all day.                                            |
| **Net terms**                       | How many days until you’re paid after invoicing. “Net 30” = up to 30 days.                                                    |
| **IP assignment**                   | A clause handing the client what you create for them. Check it doesn’t take your own work too.                                |
| **Digital nomad visa**              | A permit to reside in a country working remotely for elsewhere. Doesn’t settle where you pay tax.                             |
| **KPI (key performance indicator)** | A metric — few — tied to an objective that actually changes a decision. Twenty of them aren’t key.                            |
| **Vanity metric**                   | Always goes up and feels good, but changes no decision. Never drops, never hurts.                                             |
| **North Star metric**               | The single number that best sums up whether you create value. When two KPIs conflict, it wins.                                |
| **Leading vs lagging**              | Leading predicts (meetings booked); lagging confirms (revenue). You need both.                                                |
| **Counter-metric (guardrail)**      | Watches that improving one KPI doesn’t break something you aren’t watching.                                                   |
| **OKR**                             | A qualitative Objective + Key Results (2 to 4 numbers). Ties work to strategy.                                                |
| **Churn**                           | The rate at which your customers leave. The hole growth leaks out of.                                                         |
| **Runway**                          | Months of cash left at your current burn. When to raise or cut.                                                               |
